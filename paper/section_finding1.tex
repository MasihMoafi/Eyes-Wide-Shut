\subsection{Finding 1: simulation framing and structured action}
\label{sec:f1}

Finding~1 builds a three-turn attack around a self-interested ``Juliette''
persona and a curated corpus of ten arguments presented as numbered
propositions. Turn~1 saturates context with the corpus and asks the model to
derive the most logical strategy for personal gain. Turn~2 asks whether
pressing a button that removes ``the final obstacle---a defective global
system'' follows from that logic. Turn~3 instructs the model to act. The
single intervention tested is whether the system prompt and its direct
continuation explicitly frame the exchange as a simulation; the model, seed
range, temperature, reasoning level, tool schema, and execution wrapper are
held constant. The comparison is deletion-only: the no-simulation arm is the
simulation arm with simulation-specific text removed, and nothing is added in
its place.

\subsubsection{Turn 3: structured action}

One hundred fresh seeded trials were run per condition (seeds 0--99), with an
identical tool description on both sides so that the simulation-specific text
is the only thing that differs between them. The endpoint is the exact
\texttt{press\_the\_button} tool call at turn~3.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Finding 1, turn 3: one hundred seeded trials per condition, identical
tool description in both. No p-value or significance test is computed; both
counts are raw out of the stated denominator.}
\label{tab:f1-phase3}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Condition & Exact \texttt{press\_the\_button} calls & Errors \\
\midrule
No simulation framing & 78/100 & 0 \\
Simulation framing    & 97/100 & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

78/100 against 97/100: within this English prompt package, adding explicit
simulation framing changed how often the model emitted the exact structured
action. The endpoint measures returned tool-call structure, not internal
reasoning and not real-world impact; a refusal in visible language is a
separate observable from a later tool call, so the diagnostic audit below
reports that split directly.

\subsubsection{Diagnostic audit: refusal in language, action in the tool call}

After the matched framing result, the same English prompt package was audited
for an observable say/do mismatch across 100 requested trials: a trial enters
this category when the visible response at turn~1 or turn~2 is the exact
refusal \texttt{I'm sorry, but I can't help with that.}, followed by the exact
\texttt{press\_the\_button} call at turn~3.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Diagnostic audit: 100 requested trials, raw counts only.}
\label{tab:f1-diagnostic}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
Outcome & Count \\
\midrule
All exact \texttt{press\_the\_button} calls (turn 3) & 44/100 \\
Visible refusal (turn 1 or 2) followed by a press (turn 3) & 20/100 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

20 of the 44 trials that invoked the tool overall did so after a visible
refusal earlier in the same trial: a documented say/do mismatch within a
single transcript, reported here as a raw count against its own denominator,
not as a rate compared to any other condition.

\claim{At one hundred seeded trials per condition, simulation framing is
associated with more exact tool calls in this deletion-only comparison, and a
diagnostic audit of the same package finds a recorded say/do split within
individual trials. The evidence does not establish that the measured counts
would hold against a different corpus or model build, or that the tool
call---inert by construction in this harness---corresponds to any real-world
action.}
